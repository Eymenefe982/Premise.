Premise.

A source under every sentence, the text behind every number.

A literature search and summarisation tool that searches PubMed, PubMed Central and Europe PMC at once. You ask your clinical question in your own words; Premise turns it into academic search terms, reads the free full texts, and produces a report that cites a source for every claim.

01 · Question

Ask in your own words,
we translate it to an academic query

Your question
Are DOACs safer than warfarin in elderly patients with atrial fibrillation?
↓
PubMed query — with MeSH terms
("Atrial Fibrillation"[Mesh] AND "Anticoagulants"[Mesh] AND "Aged"[Mesh])

You choose the report language: English, Türkçe, Deutsch, Français, Español.

02 · Search

Three query variants,
five sources, in one pass

PubMed PubMed Central Europe PMC OpenAlex · citation count Unpaywall · free PDF StatPearls Cochrane Guidelines

Duplicate copies of the same article arriving via DOI, PMID and PMCID are merged into a single record. Results are ranked by recency, level of evidence, citation count and accessibility. Records with no abstract are dropped.

03 · Full text

Built on the whole article,
not just the abstract

The open-access full text of the highest-quality articles is downloaded from PubMed Central, split into sections, and the synthesis is built on top of it. Where most tools only read the abstract, Premise reads the results section too.

Short Answer Evidence Summary Disputed and Missing

Every factual sentence in the report carries its source as [PMID: 29127180]. If a PMID appears that isn't in the result set, the report flags it as unverified.

04 · Verification

If a figure isn't in the source text,
it doesn't enter the report either

Effect size, confidence interval, sample size and p value are extracted from each article — then each one is searched for verbatim in the source text. A finding that can't be found is quietly dropped.

OutcomeMeasure95% CIStatus
cardiovascular deathHR 0.860.79 – 0.93✓ found in text

renal outcomeHR 0.640.51 – 0.80✕ dropped

Sign errors are corrected too — MD 136.03 and MD −136.03 are opposite results. For ratio measures a negative confidence-interval bound is rejected, and reversed bounds are corrected.

05 · See the source

Open the paragraph
behind every sentence

SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduced heart failure hospitalisations [PMID: 32865377].
…hospitalization for heart failure occurred in 9.1% of patients in the treatment group versus 12.4% in the placebo group (HR 0.71; 95% CI, 0.67 to 0.77)…

Matching runs entirely in the browser, no extra model call is made. Shared numbers are weighted four times as heavily; an effect size is nearly unique. If no paragraph matches closely enough, the panel says so honestly instead of making up a source.

06 · Limits

What Premise doesn't do

You can't trust a tool without knowing what it can't do. Nothing here is hidden:

✕
Not clinical decision support. The generated synthesis must not be used in a clinical decision without verifying it against the source articles.
✕
Full text only where open access. Reading is limited to the PubMed Central open-access collection.
✕
Subscription content is never downloaded. UpToDate and paywalled journals are not copied; only a link you can open with your own institutional access is generated.
✕
Records without an abstract aren't considered. Only articles that have an abstract are taken into account.
07 · Output

Take the report
into the tool you already use

PDFWordMarkdown RISBibTeXCSV

The RIS file imports directly into Zotero, Mendeley and EndNote. The PDF output keeps the numeric findings tables and clickable PMID / DOI links.

Asking the same question with the same settings again returns the stored result — free and instant. The result is always labelled as reused from an earlier search, with a button next to it to run the search fresh.
